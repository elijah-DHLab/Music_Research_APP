Listening in Israel During the War

Research guide to the interactive chart explorer. This guide explains how to interpret the results, compare sources and reproduce analyses.

Spotify and YouTube represent different audiences and different measurement systems. Raw streams and views must not be added together and interpreted as a single listening volume.

Getting started

Use the global filter bar to choose one or more sources, languages, genres, countries, gender categories or time limits. The Overview, Songs, Artists and Explore tabs allow selecting a group-by dimension, a secondary split, a metric, a chart style and a time resolution. Research Questions provides ready-made analyses. Reset Filters restores the full source selection.

Comparing two, three or more categories

Choose a dimension such as Genre or Language. Use the series chooser above the chart to select any number of individual series; selected series remain visible together. Add another category with its checkbox; clear the selection to return to the top-ranking categories. Use the secondary split “Source” to show each source separately. The global Source filter may instead restrict the comparison to Spotify or YouTube only.

Time alignment and normalization

Weekly aggregation is the default common frequency: Spotify daily observations are grouped into the corresponding week and YouTube weekly observations retain their weekly bins. Monthly and yearly aggregation are also available. The original daily/weekly option is only appropriate for within-source inspection. For cross-source comparison, prefer Share within each source (%) rather than raw values; a source split can show the differences between sources. The index option compares change relative to an earlier baseline.

How to read the charts

Move over a line or table row to highlight a category and inspect the value. Click a line or a row to pin a series. Click its legend item to toggle visibility. Drag horizontally to define an additional comparison period. The Raw link leads to corresponding source rows. The CSV button exports the plotted series.

Period comparison

The Compare Periods tab supports editable period names and date ranges. It summarizes dimensions separately and highlights values with large changes. Period boundaries are stored locally in your browser; export comparisons to CSV for further analysis. Raw totals from different sources are displayed for auditing only, not as a valid cross-platform sum.

Works, recordings and chart observations

A musical work is a conceptual song; it may have multiple recordings or performances. A recording can appear on multiple platforms under different titles and identifiers. Each chart observation records its source, time, rank and count. The Data & Sources tab includes three separate exports: proposed works, recordings, and a crosswalk to the updated master list. Candidate connections need human review before being treated as verified identities.

Interpretive cautions

Ranked charts are not a complete sample of all listening. Differences between the YouTube Tracks and Videos charts may reflect platform classification rather than musical semantics. Language and genre metadata may be incomplete or inferred, and should be validated before publication. Spotify daily coverage contains gaps: there are 912 days with observations, not a continuous daily panel throughout the period. Missing dates must not be treated as zero streams.

Data, export and API

The Data & Sources tab provides links to the original spreadsheets, raw observations and the normalized song catalog. The API tab documents URL query parameters such as grp, split, m, gran, src, lang, genre, from and to. CSV outputs start with a UTF-8 BOM to ease opening in spreadsheet applications.

Offline use and publishing

Extract the ZIP first, then open site/index.html. Under file://, the website uses the bundled JavaScript data fallback. On an HTTP(S) host such as GitHub Pages, it prioritizes the smaller compressed data transfer. The interface and dataset work as a static website and do not require a database server.

Research framing and responsible interpretation

The project brief Music streaming during the Iron Swords war proposes questions about language, repertoire, change points, and different music uses during crisis. These data describe Top charts—not all listening in Israel or users’ psychological motivations.

Missing does not mean zero: the first observed date for both weekly YouTube series is October 12, 2023. YouTube cannot support a before/after October 7 comparison. Spotify has an earlier baseline but incomplete coverage overall. Zero chart entries during observed coverage only means absence from the ranked chart.

Studies informing research-question design

  • Lee et al., Unsettled Times: distinguishes music discovery (Shazam) from streaming popularity and highlights cross-platform triangulation.
  • Abramenko et al., War and Nation-Building (2026): motivates language/identity questions but does not justify identity conclusions from chart language alone.
  • Terroso-Sáenz et al. (2023): explores audio features that are not yet included in the present dataset.
  • Yeung (2023): distinguishes age of recordings from nostalgia and uses pre-period/placebo comparisons. A returning older song is not proof of nostalgic motivation.

Each question is marked measured, partial, or not answerable. Numerical summaries are preliminary descriptive calculations requiring coverage and identity validation before publication.
